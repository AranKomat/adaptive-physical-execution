# First task-level direct-controller experiments

## Result

Both single-arm ports approached the GPU, attempted to grasp it, recognized that
lift tests did not establish capture, and retried with altered depth or lateral
position. Neither produced a visually verified lift or native task success.
Failure recognition is promising, but it is not successful recovery.

Shared: PC GPU scene, Franka, seed 0, three RGB cameras, no demonstrations,
OpenRouter `openai/gpt-6-astra`, pinned `openai/flex` route, requested medium
reasoning, native grasp-weld assistance. No object poses or scorer values entered
policy context. There were no resets inside either episode and no task script
was generated by the model.

Important differences: A used anchored history, incremental Cartesian actions,
up to five targets per call, and a 30-decision ceiling. B used full history,
absolute EEF targets, up to three targets per call, and a 20-decision ceiling.
Both had a 500,000 cumulative token threshold (checked before the next request),
900-second wall limit, and a $5 local cap. Hence this is an exploratory comparison,
not a matched-exposure benchmark or an upstream-paper reproduction.

## Physical interpretation

- Both controllers can use the camera/proprioception interface to approach the part.
- Both distinguish gripper closure from a verified grasp in their public assessments.
- Direct-A tried deeper grasps, settling holds, and lateral corrections after missed lifts.
- Direct-B also reopened and changed lateral/depth targets after failed lift tests.
- Final retained RGB still shows the card in its support. Neither reaches insertion.
- These runs do not establish whether stronger reasoning, better legal camera
  calibration, different grasp orientation, or the motor prior would solve the failure.
- Controller lag appears in receipts and is acknowledged by the model. That alone
  does not prove the IK/controller is faulty; do not change physics to force success.

## High-reasoning follow-up

`astra_direct_a_high_20_20260929` used Astra Flex with requested high reasoning,
the same scene/seed and Direct-A horizon, and a 20-decision cap. It completed
20 calls and 71 control steps (4.733 simulated seconds) in 406.45 wall seconds.
Reported API cost was $0.66809500: 403,252 input tokens (362,140 cached),
9,334 output tokens including 5,424 reasoning tokens. Summed model latency
was 317.70 seconds. The run ended at the decision cap, without a runtime error.

Native score remained 0 and success false. The model reported missed lift tests;
the inspected final left-camera frame still shows the card on its support.
There is no verified grasp/lift success. Two proposed chunks were rejected without
execution: one IK nonconvergence (2.98 mm position error), and one large joint
transition caught by the continuity guard. This single bounded run does not
establish that high reasoning is generally ineffective, but it did not fix this
episode's contact/alignment problem. No further reasoning escalation is justified
by this result alone.
